Why Philippines Imports Garlic from China

The Philippines relies heavily on garlic imports to meet domestic demand, as local production accounts for only a small fraction of the 50,000+ tons consumed annually. The tropical maritime climate of the archipelago limits garlic cultivation to a few highland areas in the north, making year-round domestic supply impossible. Chinese garlic has become the primary import source for Philippine distributors and food manufacturers due to its consistent quality, competitive pricing, and reliable supply chain from Shandong Province. The close geographic proximity between China and the Philippines ensures shorter transit times and lower freight costs compared to other origins. Furthermore, Chinese garlic exporters offer the full range of sizes and packaging formats that Philippine importers require, from retail-ready 10kg cartons for supermarket chains to bulk 20kg cartons for food processing companies. The Bureau of Plant Industry (BPI) import clearance process is well-established for Chinese garlic, making the trade route efficient and predictable.

Garlic Import FAQ — Philippines

What import permit is required to bring garlic into the Philippines?
Garlic imports into the Philippines require a Plant Quarantine Clearance (PQC) issued by the Bureau of Plant Industry (BPI). Importers must also secure a Sanitary and Phytosanitary (SPS) Import Clearance from the Department of Agriculture. We provide all necessary export documents including Phytosanitary Certificate, Certificate of Origin, and fumigation certificate to support your BPI application and customs clearance at Manila, Cebu, or Davao ports.
Are there import quotas or restrictions on garlic entering the Philippines?
The Philippines implements a minimum access volume (MAV) system for garlic, with in-quota imports subject to a lower tariff rate (35%) and out-quota imports subject to a higher rate (up to 120%). Importers should verify their MAV allocation status with the Department of Agriculture. We can adjust shipment scheduling and quantities to align with your quota position and help optimize your import costs.
What is the best season to import garlic for the Philippine market?
The optimal purchasing window is July through October, following the Chinese garlic harvest in June. During this period, fresh crop garlic offers the best quality and pricing. For year-round supply, our cold storage facilities maintain garlic quality from June through March. Philippine demand typically peaks during the Christmas and New Year holiday season, so we recommend placing orders 4-6 weeks in advance to secure stock and vessel space.
