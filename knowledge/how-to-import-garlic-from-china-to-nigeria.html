How to Import Garlic from China to Nigeria: Complete 2026 Guide

A practical, step-by-step guide for Nigerian garlic importers: Tin Can Island and Apapa ports, Form M and PAAR process, ECOWAS tariff, shipping routes from Qingdao, documentation, and cost breakdown for Normal White garlic.

Published: August 12, 2026 | Reading time: 14 min

Quick Answer: To import garlic from China to Nigeria, you must register a Form M with a Nigerian bank before shipment, obtain a PAAR (Pre-Arrival Assessment Report) from Nigeria Customs, and have a Phytosanitary Certificate and Certificate of Origin from your Chinese supplier. Ocean freight from Qingdao to Lagos takes 30-35 days. The ECOWAS tariff on garlic is approximately 20% plus 7.5% VAT. Nigeria is a price-sensitive market preferring Normal White garlic in 5.0-5.5cm sizes. The standard MOQ is 1 x 40HQ Reefer Container (28MT) at -2°C to -3°C.

Nigeria is Africa's most populous country and one of the continent's largest food import markets. With over 220 million people and a rapidly growing urban population, demand for garlic — used extensively in Nigerian cooking, traditional medicine, and food processing — is substantial and rising. While Nigeria has some domestic garlic production in the northern states, local supply falls far short of national demand, making imports essential.

This guide is written for Nigerian trading companies, market distributors, and food processors who want to source garlic directly from China. It covers the Nigerian garlic market, the Form M and PAAR import process, regulatory requirements, shipping routes, documentation, and a cost breakdown comparing FOB Qingdao and CIF Lagos terms. Nigeria's import process is more complex than many markets due to the Form M/PAAR system, so understanding each step is critical.

Nigeria Garlic Market Overview

Nigeria consumes an estimated 80,000-100,000 metric tons of garlic annually. Domestic production, concentrated in northern states such as Kaduna, Kano, Sokoto, and Bauchi, supplies approximately 30,000-40,000 MT. The remainder — over 50,000 MT — is imported, primarily from China. India and some neighboring West African countries contribute smaller volumes.

Garlic is a staple ingredient in Nigerian cuisine, used in soups, stews, jollof rice, suya spice blends, and traditional medicine. It is sold in open markets (such as Mile 12 Market in Lagos, Bodija Market in Ibadan, and Kano's Kurmi Market), supermarkets, and to food processing companies that produce garlic paste, powder, and seasoning mixes. The market is highly price-sensitive, with most consumers buying garlic in small quantities from open-air vendors.

Nigeria's garlic import trade is centered in Lagos, the country's commercial capital. Both major Lagos ports — Tin Can Island and Apapa — handle garlic imports. From Lagos, garlic is distributed by road to markets across the country, with the northern cities (Kano, Kaduna, Abuja) being significant consumption hubs due to their culinary traditions and population density.

Step-by-Step Import Process

Importing garlic from China to Nigeria involves seven key steps. Nigeria's import process is distinctive because of the Form M and PAAR system, which requires advance preparation before goods are shipped. Below is the complete process.

A supplier familiar with the Nigerian import process will know how to prepare documents that satisfy the Form M and PAAR requirements, reducing the risk of delays. Look for suppliers with ISO 22000 or HACCP certification, cold storage capacity, and a verifiable export track record to Africa.

Request the supplier's business license, export qualifications, and references from existing Nigerian or West African clients. A third-party quality inspection (SGS, BV, Intertek) before shipment is strongly recommended, especially for first-time orders. Step 3: Understand Import Regulations & Tariffs

Nigeria regulates imports through the Nigeria Customs Service (NCS), the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN), and the Standards Organisation of Nigeria (SON). The import process for garlic involves several regulatory steps:

  • ECOWAS Common External Tariff (CET): Fresh garlic (HS code 0703.20.00) falls under the ECOWAS CET at approximately 20%. Nigeria adopted the ECOWAS CET framework, and the rate may be supplemented by additional levies or surcharges depending on fiscal policy.
  • Value Added Tax (VAT): A 7.5% VAT applies to imported goods, calculated on the CIF value plus duty.
  • Form M requirement: All commercial imports require a Form M, which must be registered with an authorized dealer bank before goods are shipped. The Form M declares the importer's intention to import and initiates the PAAR process.
  • PAAR (Pre-Arrival Assessment Report): After Form M registration, the Nigeria Customs Service reviews the documents and issues a PAAR, which determines the correct HS code, duty rate, and value for assessment. PAAR must be obtained before the shipment arrives at a Nigerian port.
  • NAFDAC oversight: While fresh garlic is primarily a raw agricultural product, the National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC) may regulate processed garlic products (paste, powder). Fresh garlic typically falls under phytosanitary oversight rather than NAFDAC registration.
  • SONCAP: The Standards Organisation of Nigeria Conformity Assessment Programme (SONCAP) applies to certain regulated products. While fresh agricultural produce may be exempt, importers should confirm current requirements with SON.

Step 4: Arrange Shipping & Logistics

Garlic is shipped from Qingdao Port to Lagos in 40HQ reefer containers. The voyage takes 30-35 days, passing through the South China Sea, Indian Ocean, and around the Cape of Good Hope or through the Mozambique Channel to the Gulf of Guinea. Proper cold-chain management throughout the tropical voyage is critical.

The main destination ports for garlic in Nigeria are:

  • Tin Can Island Port (Lagos): The preferred port for many garlic importers due to relatively faster container handling and less congestion than Apapa. Tin Can Island has good reefer container facilities and connections to Lagos's wholesale markets.
  • Apapa Port (Lagos): Nigeria's largest and busiest port, also known as Lagos Port Complex. While it handles significant cargo volumes, congestion can be a problem, leading to longer dwell times and higher demurrage costs. Some importers prefer Tin Can Island for this reason.

Major shipping lines on the Qingdao-Lagos route include Maersk, MSC, and COSCO. Most services involve transshipment at hubs such as Singapore, Port Klang, or Durban. Book reefer containers well in advance, as reefer capacity on the West Africa route can be limited. The reefer container must be pre-cooled and set to -2°C to -3°C with ventilation at 25-30 CBM per hour. For cold-chain best practices, see our fresh garlic storage guide.

An important logistics consideration for Nigeria: port congestion at Lagos ports is common, and container dwell times can extend beyond expected timelines. Work with a freight forwarder experienced in the Lagos port system, and have a contingency plan for extended demurrage. Some importers use bonded warehouses near the ports to store goods while awaiting clearance.

Step 5: Prepare Required Documents

Nigeria requires a comprehensive document set for customs clearance. The Form M and PAAR process means documents must be prepared and submitted before the shipment departs China. Incomplete or incorrect documents will cause significant delays. The required documents include:

  • Form M: Registered with an authorized Nigerian bank before shipment. The Form M number must appear on all shipping documents. This is the foundation of the Nigerian import process.
  • Commercial Invoice: Must match the Form M details exactly — product description, quantity, unit price, total value, and currency. Discrepancies between the invoice and Form M will cause PAAR rejection.
  • Packing List: Must show net weight, gross weight, number of packages, and container details.
  • Bill of Lading (B/L): Issued by the shipping line. Must show the Form M number and be consistent with the commercial invoice.
  • Phytosanitary Certificate: Issued by China Customs (GACC). Mandatory for plant product imports into Nigeria.
  • Certificate of Origin: Issued by CCPIT or a chamber of commerce in China. Required for tariff classification and the PAAR assessment.
  • Fumigation Certificate: Confirms pest treatment, required for phytosanitary compliance.
  • Combined Certificate of Value and Origin (CCVO): Also known as Form C16, this document combines the certificate of origin and value declaration. Required by the Central Bank of Nigeria for foreign exchange transactions.
  • PAAR: The Pre-Arrival Assessment Report issued by Nigeria Customs Service after reviewing the Form M and supporting documents. Must be obtained before vessel arrival.

Step 6: Customs Clearance in Nigeria

Upon arrival at Tin Can Island or Apapa, the shipment enters customs clearance through the Nigeria Customs Service's electronic system. The importer's licensed customs broker files the Single Goods Declaration (SGD), referencing the Form M and PAAR. Nigeria Customs uses a risk management system to route shipments for inspection — green (no inspection), yellow (document check), or red (physical examination). Food and agricultural products are frequently routed to the red channel.

If selected for physical examination, customs officers and possibly agricultural quarantine officials will inspect the garlic. This can add 3-7 days to clearance time. Port congestion may add further delays. Work with a licensed customs broker who has experience with agricultural imports and strong relationships with officials at Tin Can Island or Apapa.

Duties and taxes payable at clearance include the approximately 20% ECOWAS import duty, 7.5% VAT, and any applicable levies. These are calculated on the CIF value. Ensure your PAAR assessment is accurate to avoid disputes over valuation. After duty payment and inspection, customs issues a release order, and the container can be picked up and transported to your warehouse.

Step 7: Cold Storage & Distribution

After customs release, garlic should be moved to a storage facility quickly. In Nigeria's hot and humid climate, garlic quality deteriorates rapidly without proper storage. While cold storage facilities are less prevalent in Nigeria than in some other markets, Lagos has commercial cold storage warehouses that can accommodate garlic. Alternatively, well-ventilated, shaded storage can work for short-term holding before distribution.

From storage, garlic is distributed to major wholesale markets — Mile 12 Market (Lagos), Bodija Market (Ibadan), Kurmi Market (Kano), and other regional markets — by road transport. Northern Nigeria receives garlic via long-haul trucks from Lagos. For open-market distribution, 20kg mesh bags are the standard packaging format, as they allow easy handling and sale by the bag or by smaller quantities.

Food processors who buy garlic for paste, powder, or seasoning production may require specific quality standards. Dehydrated garlic flakes are also imported for the processing industry — see our dehydrated garlic flakes product page for more information.

Nigeria-Specific Requirements & Regulations

Nigeria has several import requirements that make its process distinctive:

  • Form M is non-negotiable: No commercial import can enter Nigeria without a Form M. It must be registered with an authorized dealer bank before the supplier ships the goods. The Form M process links the import declaration to foreign exchange allocation and the PAAR assessment.
  • PAAR timing: The PAAR must be issued before the shipment arrives in Nigeria. Submit all required documents to your bank immediately after Form M registration so the PAAR can be processed while the vessel is in transit. A late PAAR means the shipment will sit at the port accruing demurrage.
  • Foreign exchange: The Central Bank of Nigeria regulates foreign exchange for imports. Ensure your bank can allocate forex for your garlic import. Forex availability can sometimes be a constraint, so plan accordingly.
  • Document consistency: The commercial invoice, Form M, Bill of Lading, and PAAR must all have consistent information. Any discrepancy — even a minor one — can lead to clearance delays and additional costs.
  • Port congestion: Lagos ports are known for congestion. Build extra time into your supply chain planning, and consider the cost of potential demurrage when calculating your landed cost.

Shipping Routes: Qingdao to Lagos

The Qingdao-to-Lagos route is a well-established Asia-West Africa shipping lane. Understanding the route helps importers plan their supply chain and manage the long lead time.

Garlic is loaded into a 40HQ reefer container at Qingdao Port. The container is connected to reefer power on the vessel and maintained at -2°C to -3°C throughout the voyage. Most services route south through the South China Sea, with transshipment at Singapore or Port Klang, then across the Indian Ocean to the East African coast, and north through the Gulf of Guinea to Lagos.

Route Segment Transit Time
Qingdao to Singapore (transshipment) 7-10 days
Singapore to Gulf of Guinea 18-22 days
Total ocean transit (Qingdao to Lagos) 30-35 days
Tin Can Island vs Apapa Similar transit; Tin Can Island often faster berthing

Maersk is the market leader on the West Africa route with frequent sailings and reliable reefer service. MSC offers competitive pricing and good coverage. COSCO has expanded its West Africa presence. Freight rates vary by season and container availability. West Africa reefer capacity can be tight during peak periods, so book early.

FAQ: Importing Garlic from China to Nigeria

What documents are required to import garlic into Nigeria?

You need a Form M (registered with a Nigerian bank before shipment), Commercial Invoice, Packing List, Bill of Lading, Phytosanitary Certificate from China Customs, Certificate of Origin, fumigation certificate, Combined Certificate of Value and Origin (CCVO), and a PAAR (Pre-Arrival Assessment Report) from Nigeria Customs. All documents must be consistent with the Form M details to avoid clearance problems.

What is the import tariff for garlic from China to Nigeria?

Fresh garlic (HS code 0703.20.00) is subject to the ECOWAS Common External Tariff of approximately 20%, plus a 7.5% VAT. Additional levies may apply depending on current fiscal policy. Importers should verify the exact rate with the Nigeria Customs Service tariff schedule before confirming an order. The total tax burden is significant, so factor it into your pricing calculations.

What is Form M and why is it required for garlic imports to Nigeria?

Form M is a mandatory import declaration document in Nigeria. It must be registered with an authorized Nigerian bank before goods are shipped from the origin country. Form M initiates the PAAR (Pre-Arrival Assessment Report) process and is required for all commercial imports. Without a Form M, the shipment cannot be cleared through Nigerian customs, and foreign exchange cannot be allocated. Processing typically takes 1-2 weeks.

How long does it take to ship garlic from China to Nigeria?

Ocean freight from Qingdao Port to Lagos (Tin Can Island or Apapa) takes 30-35 days. Including 5-7 days for production, inspection, and loading in China, plus 5-10 days for customs clearance at Lagos ports, the total lead time is typically 6-8 weeks. Port congestion at Lagos can extend this timeline, so build buffer time into your planning.

Which garlic specifications are most popular in the Nigerian market?

Nigeria is a price-sensitive market, so Normal White garlic in 5.0cm and 5.5cm sizes is the most popular choice. Normal White garlic offers good quality at an affordable price point. Packaging is typically 20kg mesh bags for open-market distribution or 10kg cartons for supermarket channels. The market favors cost-efficient options that can be distributed through open markets and street vendors across Nigeria's states.
