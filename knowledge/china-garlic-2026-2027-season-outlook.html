A realistic read on the 2026 crop, storage inventory, export demand and the price path ahead, with practical purchasing windows for importers.

Every October, garlic importers around the world ask the same question: where are Chinese garlic prices heading this season? This outlook consolidates what we observe on the ground in Shandong, China's leading garlic export province, into a practical purchasing framework for the 2026-2027 season. One honest caveat before we begin: garlic is one of the most volatile agricultural commodities in China, and no forecast, including this one, substitutes for a live quotation. Reviewing the 2026 Crop: What Happened in June

The 2026 garlic crop in Shandong followed its standard calendar: planting in October 2025, winter dormancy, rapid growth from March, scape removal in May, and the main harvest in June 2026. After harvest, bulbs were field-cured for 2-3 weeks, then moved into cold stores at -2 to -3 degrees Celsius from July through September. You can read the full crop calendar in our China Garlic Harvest Season guide.

As is typical, the 2026 season delivered its usual mix of regional variation. Weather during the growing window (October to May) is the single biggest yield variable: a warm, dry spring tends to accelerate maturity and lift average bulb size, while late frost, excessive rain at harvest, or a cool spring can trim yields and push the size mix toward smaller calibres. For the 2026 crop, growing conditions in Shandong were broadly within the normal range, with the usual local differences in average size distribution between fields and storage operators. Overall quality entering storage, firmness, skin integrity, moisture control, was consistent with export requirements across the standard calibres we ship: 4.5cm, 5.0cm, 5.5cm, 6.0cm and 6.5cm+.

What matters most to importers is not the absolute crop number in any given year, but the relationship between three quantities: how much garlic entered storage, how fast it is being consumed by domestic and export demand, and how much area farmers plant for the next season. Those three levers set the price path.

The 2026-2027 Price Outlook: Direction, Not Destiny

Chinese garlic pricing follows a recognizable seasonal structure that repeats because the supply is physically fixed each June and then drawn down over roughly twelve months. Within that structure, prices move with storage volumes and demand. Here is how the 2026-2027 season is positioned as of October 2026:

  • Q4 2026 (now): seasonal low zone. Storage rooms are full, the crop has been sorted and stabilized, and sellers compete to move early volume. Embedded storage cost is minimal. Historically, October-December FOB levels sit measurably below the spring peak, with the gap between early-season and late-season pricing commonly in the 10-30% range depending on the year.
  • Q1 2027: gradual firming. Export demand runs steadily (Southeast Asia, the Middle East and Africa buy consistently through winter), while the Chinese New Year holiday in early 2027 slows loading operations for 2-3 weeks. Buyers who wait until after the holiday typically pay more than those who shipped before it.
  • Q2-Q3 2027: seasonal peak, then reset. As storage depletes from March onward, remaining inventory commands premium pricing, and quality scrutiny becomes critical because substandard stock starts surfacing in the market. The peak typically runs March-May, after which the June 2027 harvest resets the whole curve.

Beyond the seasonal shape, three structural factors will influence how steep this year's curve turns out:

  1. Storage inventory level. The volume of 2026-crop garlic that entered cold stores in Shandong sets total supply through May 2027. Higher entry volumes generally flatten the seasonal price curve; lower volumes steepen it. Storage entry data solidifies between September and November, which is one more reason Q4 is a good decision-making window: the information is finally visible.
  2. Export demand pace. China supplies roughly 80% of globally traded garlic, so when international demand strengthens (population growth, food industry expansion, competing-origin crop failures), Chinese stock draws down faster and prices firm earlier in the season.
  3. 2027 planting intentions. Farmers decide in October-November how much area to plant for the 2027 harvest, responding to the prices they saw this year. High 2026 prices tend to expand 2027 planting, which the market prices in during spring. Speculative storage traders watch this signal closely, and their positioning amplifies moves in both directions.

We deliberately do not publish a single "forecast number." Garlic prices routinely swing 20-50% within a season, and any specific figure quoted today would be stale by the time you read it. The responsible approach is directional analysis plus live pricing. China's Export Engine in Context

It is worth restating how concentrated the global garlic trade is. China accounts for approximately 80% of world garlic exports by volume, shipping on the order of two million tonnes or more per year to more than 100 countries. Spain, Argentina, the Netherlands and others supply meaningful volumes regionally, but no alternative origin can absorb Chinese-scale volume on short notice.

The practical consequences for importers:

  • Chinese storage inventory and pricing effectively set the world reference price. What happens in Shandong cold stores shows up in your local wholesale market within weeks.
  • When Chinese supply tightens, alternative origins are usually already sold out or significantly more expensive, so switching origins mid-season is rarely a viable hedge.
  • Import demand is structurally steady to growing, which is why the seasonal drawdown pattern repeats with such regularity.

Exchange Rates and Freight: The Hidden Half of Your Landed Cost

FOB price is only part of the story. Two external variables move your total landed cost independently of what happens in the garlic market:

  • USD/RMB exchange rate. Chinese garlic is quoted in USD, but suppliers' costs are in RMB. When the RMB weakens, exporters can accept lower dollar prices for the same RMB margin; when it strengthens, the pressure reverses. Currency moves of 3-5% can meaningfully shift quotes between the time you negotiate and the time you pay.
  • Ocean freight rates. A standard export shipment is one 40HQ reefer carrying about 28 metric tons. When 40HQ reefer rates to your destination spike, the per-ton freight cost rises proportionally, and in tight freight markets the freight component can swing total landed cost by 10-20%. Freight markets move independently of garlic markets, so always quote freight and FOB together before comparing suppliers.

Four Purchasing Strategies for the 2026-2027 Season

  1. 1. Front-load your volume in Q4. The seasonal structure rewards early action: full storage, low prices, uniform quality, and ample choice of sizes from 4.5cm through 6.5cm+. If your cash flow allows, covering 60-70% of your annual requirement between October and January historically beats averaging into the spring peak.
  2. 2. Split shipments instead of one mega-order. Multiple 40HQ reefers spread across months average your cost basis and avoid aging inventory at destination. Cold stored garlic keeps well, but your own warehouse turnover still benefits from fresh arrivals. Each container holds 28MT, so plan program volumes in container multiples.
  3. 3. Fix quality specs in the contract, not just price. In a rising market, the temptation to cut corners grows. Specify calibre, sprouting tolerance, mold limits, and third-party inspection rights in writing. On late-season (March-May) shipments, a sprouting-rate check on a 50-100 bulb sample is cheap insurance. See our cold stored garlic guide for the full inspection checklist.
  4. 4. Re-quote before every order. Garlic prices can move meaningfully in two weeks. Get an updated FOB Qingdao quote, current freight, and confirm the FX rate for your payment window before issuing a purchase order. A five-minute WhatsApp message can save a five-figure mistake.

Risks That Could Change This Outlook

Every garlic season produces surprises. The main risks to this baseline view:

  • Speculative storage behavior. Chinese cold store traders sometimes withhold stock to force prices up, or dump stock in panic. These flows can push prices off the seasonal script for weeks at a time.
  • Weather in the 2027 growing season. A freeze, flood or drought between now and June 2027 changes the next crop's size and re-prices expectations immediately.
  • Trade policy shifts. Tariffs, phytosanitary rule changes or import licensing changes in major destinations can redirect demand suddenly. Check our garlic import regulations by country guide for your market.
  • Freight and FX shocks. As covered above, these can move landed cost 10-20% independent of garlic fundamentals.

None of these risks argue against buying; they argue against complacency. The importers who consistently get the best full-year garlic economics are the ones who buy the seasonal low, keep specifications tight, and stay in weekly contact with their supplier through the season.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will garlic prices go up or down in 2027?
Within the season, the typical pattern is rising prices from Q4 lows to a March-May peak, then a reset at the June 2027 harvest. Whether the overall level is higher or lower than last year depends on storage volumes, export demand and the 2027 planted area. Track the market monthly and always confirm with a live quote.
How much garlic does China export each year?
Around 2 million tonnes per year or more, roughly 80% of global garlic export volume, shipped to over 100 countries. Because of this share, Chinese storage levels effectively set the world price benchmark.
Should I sign a fixed-price annual contract?
Fixed-price contracts protect you from seasonal increases but can lock you above the market if prices fall. A middle path many importers use: fix price and volume for Q4-Q1 shipments (near the seasonal low), then re-negotiate remaining quarters as the season unfolds.
What is the minimum order for Chinese garlic?
For export, the standard minimum is one 40HQ reefer container holding approximately 28 metric tons. This is the economical unit: smaller volumes suffer proportionally higher freight and handling costs per ton.
How often should I re-check garlic prices?
At minimum before every purchase order, and weekly if you are timing a large commitment. Chinese garlic prices can move meaningfully within two weeks during active seasons. A quick quote request via WhatsApp takes minutes and keeps your numbers current.
