China Garlic Wholesale Market Overview

China produces approximately 20-24 million metric tons of garlic annually, accounting for over 60% of global production. Of this, about 1.5-2.5 million MT are exported each year, with the remainder consumed domestically. Shandong Province is the heart of China's garlic industry, producing the majority of export-grade garlic.

The garlic harvest season in Shandong runs from late May to June. After harvest, garlic is dried, sorted, and either shipped fresh or moved to cold storage facilities. Shandong's cold storage capacity is enormous — holding tens of thousands of MT — allowing year-round supply. Fresh garlic is available from June through September, while cold-stored garlic is shipped from October through May.

China's garlic export infrastructure is well-developed, with Qingdao Port serving as the primary shipping hub. Garlic is exported to over 150 countries, with major destinations including Southeast Asia (Indonesia, Vietnam, Philippines, Thailand), South Asia (India, Bangladesh), the Middle East (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Egypt), Africa (Nigeria, South Africa), South America (Brazil), and Europe. Garlic Specification Selection Guide

Selecting the right garlic variety and size is the most important decision in bulk sourcing. The choice affects price, target market suitability, shelf life, and ultimately your profit margin. China exports two main varieties of fresh garlic:

  • Normal White Garlic: Also called "Regular White" or "Red Garlic." Has natural purple/red streaks on the outer skin. More affordable, widely used in price-sensitive markets. Ideal for wholesale markets, food processing, and daily cooking. See Normal White Garlic product page.
  • Pure White Garlic: Also called "Snow White" or "All White." Has bright, clean white skin with no purple streaks. Premium appearance commands higher prices. Ideal for retail, supermarket chains, and markets with high quality expectations. See Pure White Garlic product page.

Beyond variety, garlic is sorted by bulb diameter into the following sizes:

Size Price Level Best For
4.5cm Lowest Budget wholesale markets, food processing, dehydrated garlic production
5.0cm Low Price-sensitive markets (Philippines, Egypt, Vietnam), daily cooking
5.5cm Medium Standard wholesale, balanced price-to-quality ratio
6.0cm High Retail, supermarket chains, quality-conscious markets (Brazil, South Africa)
6.5cm+ Highest Premium retail, Saudi Arabia/UAE, hospitality sector

The price difference between sizes can be significant — 6.5cm+ garlic may cost 30-50% more than 5.0cm garlic of the same variety. Choose the size that matches your target market's expectations and willingness to pay. For a detailed size specification guide, see our garlic size specification guide.

MOQ and Container Types

The standard minimum order quantity (MOQ) for bulk garlic from China is 1 x 40HQ Reefer Container, holding approximately 28 metric tons (28,000 kg) of garlic. This is the industry standard for garlic exports and is the most cost-efficient shipping unit.

Key container specifications:

  • Container type: 40HQ (High Cube) Reefer — taller than standard 40ft, providing maximum cargo capacity.
  • Capacity: ~28MT of garlic (depends on packaging type and pallet configuration).
  • Temperature: Set at -2°C to -3°C to keep garlic dormant without freezing.
  • Ventilation: 25-30 CBM/hour to prevent moisture buildup and mold.
  • Humidity: 65-70% relative humidity, maintained by the reefer unit.

Some suppliers may accept smaller orders using a 20ft Reefer container (~14MT) or LCL (Less than Container Load), but the per-unit cost will be significantly higher — often 20-40% more per kilogram. For serious wholesale buyers, the 40HQ Reefer is the only cost-efficient option. Wholesale Price Factors: What Drives Garlic Prices

Garlic wholesale prices fluctuate significantly throughout the year and across seasons. Understanding the factors that drive price changes helps you time your purchases for maximum cost savings.

  • Seasonal cycle: Prices are lowest from June to September (post-harvest, abundant supply) and highest from December to March (off-season, cold storage inventory depleting). The price difference between peak and off-season can be 40-80%.
  • Harvest yield: Annual harvest volume directly affects prices. A bumper harvest drives prices down; a poor harvest (due to weather, disease, or reduced planting area) drives prices up. Shandong's garlic yield is influenced by winter temperature, spring rainfall, and planting area decisions.
  • Cold storage inventory: By spring, cold storage inventory levels determine market supply. High inventory = stable or declining prices. Low inventory = rising prices as buyers compete for limited stock.
  • Exchange rates: The RMB-to-USD exchange rate affects export prices. A weaker RMB makes Chinese garlic cheaper for foreign buyers; a stronger RMB increases prices.
  • Variety and size: Pure White costs more than Normal White. Larger sizes (6.0cm, 6.5cm+) command premium prices. The price gap widens during the off-season when large-size Pure White becomes scarce.
  • Order volume: Larger orders (multiple containers) may qualify for volume discounts of 3-8% per container. Long-term supply contracts can also secure preferential pricing.
  • Global demand: Surges in demand from major importers (e.g., during Ramadan, Chinese New Year, or supply disruptions in other producing countries) can temporarily raise prices.

Cold Chain Transportation Requirements

Maintaining proper cold chain conditions during ocean freight is critical to preserving garlic quality. Improper temperature management can lead to sprouting, mold, dehydration, and condensation damage — all of which reduce the value of your cargo or render it unsellable.

The standard cold chain specifications for garlic export are:

  • Temperature: -2°C to -3°C. This range keeps garlic dormant without freezing the bulbs. Freezing (below -4°C) damages cell structure and ruins garlic quality. Temperatures above 0°C allow garlic to begin metabolic activity, leading to sprouting.
  • Ventilation: 25-30 CBM/hour. Garlic respires during storage, producing heat, moisture, and gases. Proper ventilation removes this moisture and prevents condensation that causes mold.
  • Humidity: 65-70% relative humidity. Too low causes dehydration and weight loss; too high promotes mold and root growth.
  • Pre-cooling: Garlic must be pre-cooled to the target temperature before container loading. Loading warm garlic into a cold container causes condensation and temperature instability.

Work with suppliers who have experience in reefer container management and can provide temperature monitoring records. For detailed storage and preservation information, see our fresh garlic storage guide and garlic shelf life and preservation guide.

Payment Methods and Terms

Understanding payment options helps you manage risk and build trust with your Chinese supplier. The two most common payment methods for bulk garlic trade are:

Method Structure Pros & Cons
T/T 30/70 30% advance, 70% against B/L copy Simple, low cost, fast. Risk: 30% advance at risk before shipment
L/C at sight Bank guarantees payment on document presentation More secure for buyer. Higher bank fees, slower process

For first-time orders, consider starting with a smaller trial order under T/T terms to verify supplier reliability, then transitioning to regular orders. For large orders (>3 containers), L/C at sight provides additional security. Some suppliers may also accept DP (Documents against Payment) or DA (Documents against Acceptance), but these are less common in garlic trade. Always clarify payment terms in the proforma invoice before placing an order.

Quality Inspection Standards

Quality inspection is your safeguard against receiving substandard garlic. The most reliable approach is to hire a third-party inspection agency — such as SGS, BV (Bureau Veritas), or Intertek — to inspect the garlic before shipment. The inspection provides an independent, objective assessment of quality and protects you from disputes.

Standard quality parameters for bulk garlic:

Parameter Standard Notes
Size accuracy 95%+ bulbs meet specified diameter Undersized bulbs reduce market value
Moisture content < 65% High moisture causes mold and sprouting
Mold rate < 3% Moldy garlic is unsellable
Sprouting rate < 2% Sprouted garlic has reduced shelf life
Mechanical damage < 5% Cut or bruised bulbs deteriorate faster
Foreign matter < 0.5% Dirt, roots, stems, stones

The cost of third-party inspection ($200-400 per container) is a small investment compared to the value of a 28MT container ($8,000-$25,000+). The inspection report also serves as evidence in case of disputes with the supplier.

How to Get the Best Wholesale Price

Securing the best wholesale price requires strategic timing, volume commitment, and supplier relationship management. Here are proven strategies:

  • Buy during harvest season (June-September): Prices are at their annual lowest during this period. If you have cold storage capacity, stock up during this window for year-round supply at the lowest average cost.
  • Order multiple containers: Volume discounts of 3-8% per container are common for orders of 3+ containers. Negotiate volume pricing upfront.
  • Sign long-term supply contracts: Annual or semi-annual contracts with fixed or formula-based pricing provide cost stability and may secure preferential rates. Suppliers value predictable demand and reward it with better pricing.
  • Choose FOB terms if you have freight expertise: FOB Qingdao allows you to negotiate your own ocean freight, potentially saving $200-800 per container compared to the supplier's CIF markup.
  • Be flexible on size: If your market accepts 5.0cm garlic instead of 5.5cm, the price savings can be 10-20%. Match the size to your market's actual needs rather than over-specifying.
  • Compare quotes from multiple suppliers: Obtain quotes from 3-5 verified suppliers for the same specifications. This gives you market intelligence and negotiating leverage. But remember — the lowest price is not always the best value if quality is compromised.

Avoiding Common Sourcing Pitfalls

The bulk garlic trade, like any international commodity business, has its share of risks. Here are the most common pitfalls and how to avoid them:

  • Quality bait-and-switch: Some suppliers show high-quality samples but ship lower-grade garlic. Prevention: Always conduct pre-shipment inspection through SGS, BV, or Intertek. Include quality specifications in the contract with clear acceptance criteria.
  • Size shorting: Garlic sorted as 5.5cm may contain a significant percentage of 5.0cm bulbs. Prevention: Specify size tolerance (e.g., 95% minimum at stated size) in the contract and verify through inspection.
  • Price manipulation: Suppliers may quote a low garlic price but inflate packaging, loading, or documentation fees. Prevention: Request an itemized cost breakdown and compare total FOB cost, not just the per-kg garlic price.
  • Fake suppliers: Online B2B platforms may list fraudulent "suppliers" who take advance payments and disappear. Prevention: Verify business licenses, visit the supplier (or hire a local agent), check export records, and never pay 100% in advance.
  • Reefer container failure: Temperature deviations during transit can ruin the cargo. Prevention: Use reputable shipping lines, request temperature monitoring data, and consider cargo insurance.
  • Documentation errors: Incorrect or incomplete documents cause customs delays, fines, or cargo seizure. Prevention: Work with experienced suppliers and freight forwarders. Double-check all documents before shipment.
  • Payment fraud: Suppliers may request payment to a personal account or a different company name. Prevention: Always verify the supplier's official bank account details against their registered company name. Use L/C for large orders.

Cost Breakdown: Understanding FOB vs CIF

When comparing supplier quotes, you will encounter two main Incoterms: FOB (Free On Board) and CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight). Understanding the difference is essential for accurate cost comparison.

  • FOB Qingdao: The supplier's price includes garlic, packaging, loading, China customs clearance, and port charges at Qingdao. You arrange and pay for ocean freight, insurance, and destination port charges. FOB gives you control over shipping and is typically cheaper if you have good freight forwarder relationships.
  • CIF [Destination Port]: The supplier's price includes everything in FOB plus ocean freight and marine insurance, delivered to your destination port. CIF is more convenient, especially for first-time importers, but the supplier adds a markup on freight. Compare the CIF quote with your own FOB + freight estimate to see if the markup is reasonable.
