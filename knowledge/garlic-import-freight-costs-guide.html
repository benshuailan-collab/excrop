Garlic Freight Costs from China: 40HQ Reefer Rates from Qingdao Explained

How reefer freight for garlic is really priced: base ocean rate plus surcharges, the five forces that move the market, transit references from Qingdao to the world's garlic ports, and who pays what under FOB, CIF, and DAP.

Published: October 11, 2026 | Reading time: 12 min

Quick Answer: Garlic from Shandong ships in 40HQ reefer containers (28MT per unit) at -2°C to -3°C, loaded at Qingdao. The freight cost is the base ocean rate plus surcharges — BAF (bunker/fuel), peak season surcharge (PSS), and reefer-specific fees — plus local charges at both ends. Rates swing with season, fuel, and capacity, and quotes are typically valid for only about 14 days. Transit references from Qingdao: Jebel Ali 22-28 days, Jeddah 20-25 days, Santos 35-40 days, Tin Can 30-35 days, Hai Phong 8-12 days, Tanjung Priok 14-18 days, Durban 28-32 days. Because the market moves, treat any published number as a planning reference and confirm a live CIF or FOB-plus-freight quote before booking.

After the garlic price itself, ocean freight is the biggest line in any garlic importer's landed cost — and the least stable. A 40HQ reefer rate on the same lane can move by hundreds of US dollars between two sailings a few weeks apart, which is why two importers buying identical garlic at identical FOB prices can land it at noticeably different cost. Understanding how reefer freight is structured — and what moves it — turns freight from a surprise into a managed cost.

Why Garlic Must Travel in a Reefer — Not a Dry Container

Fresh garlic is a living, dormant bulb. Its dormancy holds only while it stays cold; at ambient temperature it breaks within days to weeks, and the bulb sprouts. Once sprouting begins, the cargo loses commercial value fast — sprouted garlic is discounted heavily or rejected outright at destination wholesale markets.

That is why garlic is exported in 40HQ high-cube reefer (refrigerated) containers set at -2°C to -3°C with controlled ventilation. At that temperature band the bulbs stay dormant without freezing, and a 20-40 day ocean voyage passes without quality loss. The ventilation setting matters too — garlic respires, and trapped gases and moisture accelerate mold.

Factor 40HQ Reefer (-2 to -3°C) Dry Container (ambient)

The comparison makes the economics obvious: the reefer premium is a fraction of the cargo's value, while a sprouted container is close to a total loss. For how a reefer is actually stuffed, see the container loading guide and what fits in a 40HQ.

What Makes Up a Reefer Freight Quote

When a carrier or forwarder quotes a "freight rate" for a Qingdao departure, the number usually covers the base ocean freight only. The all-in container cost is the base plus a stack of surcharges and local charges:

  • Base ocean freight: the headline rate for moving the box from Qingdao to the destination port. This is the number that moves most with the market.
  • BAF (bunker adjustment factor): the fuel-linked surcharge. It tracks bunker prices and can be revised with short notice when oil moves.
  • PSS (peak season surcharge): imposed during seasonal demand peaks — on China export trades, most predictably in the weeks before Chinese New Year and ahead of year-end holidays.
  • Reefer-specific fees: reefer equipment carries its own surcharge layers — plugged-cargo monitoring, sometimes a separate reefer surcharge on certain trades, and genset/fuel for the refrigeration unit on some routings.
  • Origin local charges (Qingdao): THC (terminal handling), documentation, VGM/weighing, seal, and customs-filing fees.
  • Destination local charges: destination THC, port dues, and — critically for reefer cargo — any plug-in and monitoring fees at the destination terminal, plus demurrage/detention if the container overstays its free time.

When comparing supplier quotes, always ask what the freight figure includes: a "CIF Jebel Ali" price that already includes freight, and an FOB price to which you add freight yourself, are only comparable when the surcharge scope is identical. The 5 Factors That Move Reefer Rates

  1. 1. Seasonality of the trade lane. China's export calendar creates predictable rate waves: the strongest is the 2-3 weeks before Chinese New Year (every sector ships at once), plus pre-Ramadan demand on Middle East lanes and Q4 pre-holiday volume to the Americas and Europe. Booking inside these windows costs more, sometimes meaningfully more.
  2. 2. Fuel prices (BAF). Bunker costs are a large share of operating cost. When oil rises, carriers adjust BAF within weeks, lifting all-in quotes even when base rates are flat.
  3. 3. Capacity and equipment supply-demand. Carriers add or remove vessel capacity per lane, and reefer containers themselves are a constrained equipment pool. When demand for reefer space exceeds equipment at Qingdao, rates rise and cargo can be rolled to a later sailing.
  4. 4. Destination port congestion. When a destination port backs up, carriers recover schedule cost through congestion surcharges, and free time at the terminal shrinks — raising the importer's demurrage exposure even when the ocean rate looks normal.
  5. 5. Reefer container positioning (balance box movements). Carriers must physically reposition empty reefer boxes to where export demand is. When reefer demand is strong in other regions, the cost of positioning equipment into China gets priced into Qingdao export rates. This is why reefer rates can diverge from dry-box rates on the same lane for reasons that have nothing to do with your cargo.

The practical implication of all five: freight is not a fixed constant you can budget once a year. It is a moving input that should be re-quoted per order cycle — with one eye on the seasonal calendar.

Why Freight Quotes Expire in About 14 Days

A common frustration for first-time buyers: you receive a CIF quote on the 1st of the month, decide on the 20th, and the freight component has changed. This is not a negotiation tactic — it is how the ocean market works. Carriers price space dynamically: fuel-linked BAF components are revised on short cycles, capacity allocations are re-balanced monthly, and surcharges are added or withdrawn as demand shifts. A quoted rate is the carrier's commitment to hold that price and that equipment for a booking window — typically around 14 days.

For garlic buyers, the takeaway is a workflow habit: request your CIF or FOB-plus-freight quote when you are actually ready to book, not weeks in advance as a "budget number," and treat quotes older than two weeks as expired. This is also why our own quotations state freight validity explicitly — the garlic price component can hold longer than the freight component. For the same reason, published freight indices are planning references, not booking prices.

FOB vs CIF vs DAP: Who Pays What

Incoterm Seller (exporter) pays Buyer (importer) pays Typical Use
FOB Qingdao Cargo, export clearance, loading on vessel at Qingdao Ocean freight, insurance, destination charges, duties, clearance Buyers with own freight contracts / forwarders
CIF destination port Cargo + ocean freight + insurance to named port (risk passes at loading) Destination charges, duties, clearance, inland haulage Most first-time and mid-size importers
DAP named place Everything through delivery to named place (duty unpaid) Import duties, taxes, customs formalities Established programs with local delivery needs

Two points buyers frequently miss. First, under CIF the cost of freight is the seller's but the risk of the voyage transfers to the buyer once the container is loaded at Qingdao — which is why insurance is built into CIF and why understanding the reefer settings protects you regardless of who booked the box. Second, whichever Incoterm you use, the destination charges and duties are always yours: no Incoterm makes the seller pay your import duties. Seven Practical Ways to Cut Freight Cost Without Cutting Quality

  • Book early, not last-minute. Space booked 3-4 weeks out is priced better than panic bookings, and early booking eliminates the cost of a rolled sailing (a week of delay on your sales calendar).
  • Avoid the pre-CNY peak where possible. If your market calendar allows, shifting a sailing from late January (peak) to March (post-holiday softness) often buys a meaningfully lower rate. The exception is seasonal-deadline cargo like Ramadan shipments — for those, booking early inside the peak is the right trade-off (see our Ramadan timeline guide).
  • Consolidate volume into fewer, fuller bookings. Booking two or three containers on one sailing and one booking number usually prices better per box than scattered single-container bookings, and simplifies destination handling.
  • Compare FOB-plus-own-freight against CIF. If you run regular volume, a freight contract with a forwarder or NVOCC on your main lane can undercut CIF quotes. If you import occasionally, CIF's simplicity usually wins on total transaction cost.
  • Watch the rate cycle and time your stock builds. Freight, like garlic itself, has seasonal low points. Importers who build cold-store inventory during freight softness (typically post-CNY, Q2) and sell through peak months capture both a freight and a price advantage.
  • Manage destination free time aggressively. Demurrage and detention on reefer boxes are punitive per day. Pre-arrange clearance and cold-store offloading before the vessel arrives; the cheapest freight saving is not paying avoidable detention.
  • Choose destination ports with congestion sense. When your market has two gateways (Santos vs Paranaguá, Jeddah vs Dammam), let current congestion — not habit — pick the port, and remember transshipment hubs can add both time and damage risk on reefer cargo.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is reefer freight from Qingdao to the Middle East or Brazil?

Rates move too much week to week for any published figure to stay reliable. As a structural point, expect a reefer 40HQ to price above a dry 40HQ on the same lane, expect the pre-Chinese-New-Year weeks to be the most expensive window, and expect quotes to be valid for about 14 days. For budgeting, request a live quote for your exact port pair and sailing week — that is the only number you can plan on.

Is CIF or FOB cheaper for garlic imports?

Neither is structurally cheaper — CIF embeds the seller's freight cost plus a coordination margin; FOB leaves freight to you. Occasional importers usually find CIF cheaper in total transaction cost (one counterparty, no booking risk). Regular importers with freight contracts on their main lane often land FOB cheaper. Compare both on the same sailing week; the FOB/CIF calculation guide shows the math.

Why is freight higher before Chinese New Year?

Every Chinese export sector — not just agriculture — pushes cargo out before factories close for the holiday, roughly 2-3 weeks of compressed demand for vessels, containers, trucking, and port labor. Carriers respond with peak season surcharges, and space sells out. After the holiday, demand drops and rates typically soften before recovering.

Does the freight rate include destination charges and duties?

No. Ocean freight (under CIF or FOB-plus-freight) ends at the destination port's quay. Destination terminal handling, port storage, customs clearance, and import duties/taxes are always the importer's responsibility under FOB, CIF, and DAP alike. Budget these explicitly — reefer demurrage and destination THC are common budget gaps for first-time importers.
