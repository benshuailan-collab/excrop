China Garlic Price Trends & Market Forecast 2026

A comprehensive analysis of factors driving garlic prices in China, seasonal patterns, and actionable strategies for importers to secure the best wholesale pricing.

Quick Answer: China garlic export prices in 2026 typically range from $700 to $1,200 per metric ton FOB Qingdao, depending on variety, size, and season. Prices are lowest during the June-August harvest season and rise 15-30% during the December-February peak demand period. Importers can save 10-20% by ordering during harvest season and securing volume contracts with origin suppliers.

Key Factors Affecting China Garlic Prices

China produces over 70% of the world's garlic supply, making it the single most influential market for global garlic pricing. Understanding the factors that drive price movements can help importers time their purchases and negotiate better terms with suppliers. The following are the primary factors that determine garlic export prices from Shandong, China's largest garlic-producing region.

1. Planting Area and Yield

Each year, the total planting area in Shandong and other major producing provinces directly impacts supply. When farmers reduce planting area due to low prices in the previous season, supply tightens and prices rise. Conversely, high prices encourage expanded planting, which can lead to oversupply 9-10 months later at harvest. Weather conditions during the growing season (October to May) also affect yields, with extreme cold or drought reducing output.

2. Cold Storage Costs

After harvest in June, garlic is stored in temperature-controlled cold storage facilities at -1°C to -3°C. Storage costs accumulate monthly, adding approximately $20-40 per ton per quarter to the base price. This is why garlic is cheapest in July-August (fresh from harvest) and becomes progressively more expensive through the winter and spring months. By March-April, cold storage garlic can cost 25-35% more than at harvest time.

3. Export Demand from Major Importers

Indonesia, Vietnam, Malaysia, the Philippines, and Saudi Arabia are among China's largest garlic export destinations. When these countries increase imports, prices rise. Demand typically peaks before major holidays such as Ramadan (for Middle Eastern and Southeast Asian markets) and Chinese New Year. Importers who anticipate these demand surges can lock in lower prices by ordering 4-6 weeks in advance.

4. Variety and Size Premium

Pure white garlic commands a 5-15% premium over purple skin garlic due to its preferred appearance in Middle Eastern markets. Larger sizes (6.0cm and 6.5cm+) carry a 10-20% premium over smaller sizes (4.5cm-5.0cm) because they are more desirable in retail markets. Dehydrated garlic flakes are priced differently, typically $1,500-$2,500 per ton, as they are a processed product with different cost structures.

How to Get the Best Garlic Export Price

Beyond timing your purchase correctly, several strategies can help you secure more competitive pricing from Chinese garlic suppliers:

  • Buy direct from origin suppliers in Shandong rather than trading companies in Shanghai or Guangzhou, which add 5-15% markup.
  • Order during harvest season (June-August) when prices are 15-30% lower than the winter peak.
  • Commit to annual volume contracts for better per-unit pricing and guaranteed supply during peak demand periods.
  • Be flexible on variety — if your market accepts purple skin garlic, you can save 5-10% compared to pure white.
  • Choose FOB terms if you have your own freight forwarder, as CIF terms include the supplier's freight margin.
  • Consolidate orders — a 40-foot container (approx. 28MT) has a lower per-ton logistics cost than a 20-foot container.

2026 Market Outlook

For 2026, garlic prices are expected to remain relatively stable compared to 2025, with moderate upward pressure in the second half. Key trends to watch include:

  • Stable planting area in Shandong, suggesting steady supply.
  • Growing demand from African markets (Nigeria, South Africa) and Southeast Asia (Indonesia, Philippines).
  • Rising cold storage and labor costs, contributing to gradual price increases outside harvest season.
  • Increased demand for value-added products like dehydrated garlic from food manufacturers worldwide.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the current price of garlic per ton in China?
Garlic prices in China fluctuate based on season, variety, and size. For 2026, wholesale prices for export-grade garlic from Shandong typically range from $700 to $1,200 per metric ton FOB Qingdao. Purple skin garlic is generally priced 5-10% lower than pure white garlic. Contact suppliers directly for real-time quotes.
When is garlic cheapest to buy from China?
Garlic prices are typically lowest during and immediately after the harvest season, from June to August. During this period, fresh garlic enters the market in large volumes, driving prices down. Prices tend to rise from November onwards as cold storage costs accumulate.
Why do garlic prices fluctuate so much?
Garlic prices are influenced by planting area changes, weather conditions during growing season, cold storage capacity, export demand from major importing countries, and speculative stockpiling. China produces over 70% of the world's garlic, so any supply disruption has global price impact.
