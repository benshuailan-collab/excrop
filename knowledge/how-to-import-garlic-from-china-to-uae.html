How to Import Garlic from China to UAE: Complete 2026 Guide

A practical, step-by-step guide for UAE garlic importers: Jebel Ali and Khalifa ports, GCC tariff, ESMA food safety registration, shipping routes from Qingdao, documentation, and cost breakdown for Dubai garlic wholesale.

Published: August 12, 2026 | Reading time: 13 min

Quick Answer: To import garlic from China to the UAE, you need a customs registration code, ESMA food product conformity registration, and a Phytosanitary Certificate plus Certificate of Origin from your Chinese supplier. Ocean freight from Qingdao to Jebel Ali takes 22-28 days. The GCC Common External Tariff on garlic is approximately 10%, plus 5% VAT. UAE buyers prefer Pure White garlic in 5.5-6.5cm+ sizes. The standard MOQ is 1 x 40HQ Reefer Container (28MT) shipped at -2°C to -3°C.

The United Arab Emirates is a major hub for food imports in the Middle East, and garlic is no exception. With a population of nearly 10 million, a massive hospitality sector, and a thriving re-export trade to neighboring Gulf and East African countries, the UAE imports the vast majority of its garlic from China. The country's domestic agriculture is minimal due to its arid climate, making garlic imports essential for both domestic consumption and regional distribution.

This guide is written for UAE-based trading companies, supermarket chains, hotel supply distributors, and re-export merchants who want to source garlic directly from China. It covers the UAE garlic market, the full import process, regulatory requirements, shipping routes, documentation, and a cost breakdown comparing FOB Qingdao and CIF Jebel Ali terms.

UAE Garlic Market Overview

The UAE imports approximately 40,000-50,000 metric tons of garlic annually. Domestic garlic production is negligible — the country's hot, arid climate is unsuitable for commercial garlic cultivation. China supplies the majority of UAE garlic imports, with smaller volumes coming from India, Egypt, and Spain. The UAE also functions as a major re-export hub, with significant volumes of imported garlic flowing onward to Saudi Arabia, Oman, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, and East African countries like Somalia and Kenya.

Dubai is the primary garlic distribution center. The Dubai Fruit & Vegetable Market in Al Warsan handles large volumes of fresh produce, including garlic, for wholesale distribution across the emirate. Abu Dhabi's Mina Wholesale Market serves a similar function for the capital. Garlic demand peaks during Ramadan and the festive seasons, when home cooking and hospitality consumption surge.

The UAE's hospitality industry — one of the largest in the world per capita — drives demand for premium-grade garlic. Hotels, restaurants, and catering companies (HoReCa) require consistently high-quality garlic with uniform bulbs and long shelf life. This makes Pure White garlic from China the preferred variety, as its clean appearance and storage characteristics suit the premium channel.

Why Import Garlic from China?

China is the world's largest garlic producer, and Shandong Province produces the majority of China's export-grade garlic. For UAE importers, sourcing directly from China offers several advantages:

  • Consistent year-round supply: Shandong's cold storage infrastructure ensures garlic availability 12 months a year, despite the June harvest cycle. This is critical for the UAE market where demand is continuous.
  • Superior quality: Shandong's climate and soil produce garlic with tight skin, plump cloves, and excellent dormancy — ideal for the 22-28 day voyage to the UAE and subsequent storage in the Gulf's hot climate.
  • Price advantage: Chinese garlic is significantly more affordable than alternatives from Spain or Egypt, even after factoring in ocean freight and 10% GCC tariff.
  • Premium Pure White availability: The UAE market demands Pure White garlic, and Shandong is the primary source of export-grade Pure White in large quantities.
  • Re-export potential: Garlic imported into the UAE can be re-exported to GCC and East African markets, often benefitting from preferential trade arrangements.

Step-by-Step Import Process

Importing garlic from China to the UAE involves seven key steps. The UAE has a relatively efficient customs process, but food safety compliance through ESMA adds a layer of preparation. Below is the complete process.

Step 1: Find a Reliable Chinese Garlic Supplier

Begin by identifying Chinese garlic exporters with proven experience shipping to the Middle East. A supplier that regularly exports to the UAE will understand the market's preference for Pure White garlic in larger sizes, the packaging formats that suit Dubai's distribution channels, and the documentation standards required by UAE customs. Look for suppliers with ISO 22000 or HACCP certification, cold storage capacity of 5,000+ MT, and a verifiable export track record.

Request the supplier's business license, export qualifications, and references from existing UAE or GCC clients. You can also arrange a third-party quality inspection (SGS, BV, Intertek) before shipment to independently verify garlic quality and size. Step 3: Understand Import Regulations & Tariffs

The UAE regulates food imports through multiple authorities. Understanding the regulatory framework ensures smooth customs clearance:

  • GCC Common External Tariff: Fresh garlic (HS code 0703.20.00) carries a GCC tariff of approximately 10%. The UAE periodically reviews tariffs on essential food items and may apply reductions. Always verify the current rate with the UAE Federal Customs Authority.
  • VAT: A 5% Value Added Tax applies to garlic at the point of import, calculated on the CIF value plus duty.
  • ESMA registration: The Emirates Authority for Standardization and Metrology requires food product conformity assessment. Importers must register garlic products and obtain a conformity certificate before customs clearance. This ensures the product meets UAE food safety standards.
  • Municipality food safety: Dubai Municipality (for Dubai imports) or Abu Dhabi Food Control Authority (for Abu Dhabi imports) conduct food safety inspections. Each shipment may be sampled at the port for laboratory testing.
  • Customs code registration: Importers must have a valid customs registration code, obtained through Dubai Trade (for Jebel Ali) or Abu Dhabi Customs (for Khalifa Port). This code is required to file electronic import declarations.

Step 4: Arrange Shipping & Logistics

Garlic is shipped from Qingdao Port in Shandong to the UAE in 40HQ reefer containers. The voyage is shorter than routes to South America or Africa, but proper temperature management remains essential, especially given the extreme heat at UAE ports during summer.

The main destination ports for garlic in the UAE are:

  • Jebel Ali Port (Dubai): The largest port in the Middle East and the primary entry point for garlic imports into the UAE. Operated by DP World, Jebel Ali has excellent reefer container facilities and direct connections to the Dubai Fruit & Vegetable Market. Most garlic imports enter through Jebel Ali.
  • Khalifa Port (Abu Dhabi): The primary port for Abu Dhabi, with growing container handling capacity. Importers serving the Abu Dhabi market and the western UAE may prefer Khalifa Port to reduce inland trucking distances.

Major shipping lines on the Qingdao-UAE route include Maersk, MSC, COSCO, and Hapag-Lloyd. Transit times are 22-28 days to Jebel Ali and 24-30 days to Khalifa Port. Direct services are available with most carriers, though some involve transshipment at Singapore or Port Klang. Book reefer containers in advance, especially before Ramadan when demand surges.

The reefer container must be pre-cooled and set to -2°C to -3°C with ventilation at 25-30 CBM per hour. Upon arrival at Jebel Ali or Khalifa, the container must be promptly connected to reefer power or unpacked into cold storage. UAE summer port temperatures can exceed 45°C, so minimizing the time garlic spends in ambient conditions is critical. For cold-chain best practices, see our fresh garlic storage guide.

Step 5: Prepare Required Documents

The UAE requires a complete and accurate document set for customs clearance. Missing documents will result in storage charges at Jebel Ali or Khalifa Port. The required documents include:

  • Commercial Invoice: Must include seller and buyer details, product description, HS code, quantity, unit price, total value, Incoterms, and currency. Attested by the chamber of commerce in China.
  • Packing List: Must show net weight, gross weight, number of cartons/bags, and container number.
  • Bill of Lading (B/L): Issued by the shipping line. For L/C payments, must comply with letter of credit terms.
  • Phytosanitary Certificate: Issued by China Customs (GACC). Mandatory for all plant product imports into the UAE.
  • Certificate of Origin: Issued by CCPIT or a chamber of commerce in China. Used for tariff classification and may be required for re-export documentation.
  • Fumigation Certificate: Confirms pest treatment, required for phytosanitary compliance.
  • ESMA conformity certificate: Confirms the garlic product meets UAE food safety standards.
  • Health Certificate: Some shipments may require a health certificate from the Chinese authorities, depending on the municipality's requirements.

Step 6: Customs Clearance in the UAE

UAE customs clearance is generally efficient, with electronic declaration systems at Jebel Ali (through Dubai Trade's Mira system) and Khalifa Port. The importer or their customs broker files the import declaration, submits all documents, and pays the applicable duties and VAT. Most declarations are processed within 24-48 hours if documents are complete.

Food shipments are subject to inspection by Dubai Municipality (at Jebel Ali) or Abu Dhabi Food Control Authority (at Khalifa Port). Inspectors may sample the garlic for laboratory testing, particularly for pesticide residues and microbial contamination. If test results are clear, the shipment is released. If issues are found, the shipment may be held, re-exported, or destroyed.

Work with a customs broker experienced in food imports to navigate the process efficiently. A good broker will pre-check your documents, ensure ESMA registration is in order, and coordinate with municipality inspectors to minimize clearance time.

Step 7: Cold Storage & Distribution

After customs release, garlic should be moved immediately to a cold storage facility. The UAE has substantial cold storage capacity near Jebel Ali and in Dubai's Al Warsan area, where the Fruit & Vegetable Market is located. Arrange cold storage before the vessel arrives to avoid demurrage and container detention charges, which can accumulate quickly at UAE ports.

From cold storage, garlic is distributed to the Dubai Fruit & Vegetable Market, Abu Dhabi Mina Market, supermarket chains (such as Carrefour, Lulu, Spinneys), hotel supply companies, and re-export channels to neighboring GCC countries. Maintain the cold chain at 0°C to -1°C during storage and distribution. In the UAE's climate, breaking the cold chain even briefly can drastically reduce shelf life.

For re-export, garlic can be trucked to other GCC countries via the UAE's excellent road network, or re-shipped by sea to East African markets. The UAE's free trade zones also offer opportunities for value-added processing, such as garlic peeling or chopping, before re-export.

UAE-Specific Requirements & Regulations

The UAE has specific import requirements that differ from other markets:

  • ESMA conformity: Food product registration under ESMA is mandatory. Importers must submit product specifications, lab test reports, and supplier certifications. The conformity certificate must be valid at the time of import.
  • Arabic labeling: Pre-packaged garlic for retail sale must have labels in Arabic (and optionally English), including product name, country of origin, net weight, production date, expiry date, and importer details. Bulk garlic in mesh bags or cartons for wholesale does not require retail labels.
  • Halal compliance: Fresh garlic is inherently halal and does not require halal certification. However, any processed garlic products (such as garlic paste or sauce) may require halal certification for the UAE market.
  • Municipality inspection: Each emirate has its own food safety authority. Dubai Municipality oversees Dubai imports, while Abu Dhabi Food Control Authority handles Abu Dhabi. Importers must ensure compliance with the specific emirate where they clear customs.
  • Re-export documentation: If you plan to re-export garlic to other GCC countries, ensure your original import documents are properly attested and maintain a clear chain of custody. GCC countries may require original certificates for re-imported goods.

Shipping Routes: Qingdao to Jebel Ali

The Qingdao-to-Jebel Ali route is one of the most established Asia-Middle East shipping lanes. Multiple carriers offer regular weekly sailings, providing flexibility in scheduling. Understanding the route helps importers plan their supply chain effectively.

Garlic is loaded into a 40HQ reefer container at Qingdao Port. The container is connected to reefer power on the vessel and maintained at -2°C to -3°C throughout the voyage. Most services route south through the South China Sea, then west across the Indian Ocean to the Strait of Hormuz and into the Arabian Gulf. Direct services are available with Maersk, MSC, COSCO, and Hapag-Lloyd, with transit times of 22-28 days.

Route Segment Transit Time
Qingdao to South China Sea 3-5 days
South China Sea to Indian Ocean 5-8 days (may include transshipment at Singapore/Port Klang)
Indian Ocean to Strait of Hormuz 8-12 days
Total ocean transit (Qingdao to Jebel Ali) 22-28 days
Qingdao to Khalifa Port 24-30 days

Maersk offers the most frequent sailings with reliable reefer service. MSC provides competitive pricing and good coverage. COSCO has expanded its Middle East services significantly. Hapag-Lloyd is a solid option for importers who value German-engineered reefer container technology. Freight rates vary by season — rates typically increase before Ramadan as demand for food imports surges across the Gulf.

FAQ: Importing Garlic from China to the UAE

What documents are required to import garlic into the UAE?

You need a Commercial Invoice (attested by chamber of commerce), Packing List, Bill of Lading, Phytosanitary Certificate from China Customs, Certificate of Origin, and a fumigation certificate. You also need ESMA food product conformity registration and a valid customs registration code (through Dubai Trade for Jebel Ali or Abu Dhabi Customs for Khalifa Port).

What is the import tariff for garlic from China to the UAE?

Fresh garlic (HS code 0703.20.00) is subject to the GCC Common External Tariff of approximately 10%. A 5% VAT also applies at import. The UAE periodically reviews food tariffs and may apply reductions, so verify the current rate with the UAE Federal Customs Authority before confirming an order.

How long does it take to ship garlic from China to the UAE?

Ocean freight from Qingdao Port to Jebel Ali (Dubai) takes 22-28 days, with most direct services at 24-26 days. Including 5-7 days for production, inspection, and loading in China, plus 2-3 days for customs clearance at Jebel Ali, the total lead time is typically 4-5 weeks. This is significantly shorter than routes to South America or Africa.

Which garlic specifications are most popular in the UAE market?

UAE buyers strongly prefer Pure White garlic in 5.5cm, 6.0cm, and 6.5cm+ sizes. The Dubai market values premium appearance for retail and hospitality channels. Packaging is typically 10kg cartons for retail and hotel supply, or 20kg mesh bags for the Dubai Fruit & Vegetable Market wholesale trade.

Do I need ESMA registration to import food products like garlic into the UAE?

Yes. ESMA (Emirates Authority for Standardization and Metrology) requires food product conformity assessment under the UAE food product registration scheme. Importers must register products and obtain conformity certificates before customs clearance. A UAE-based customs broker or product registration consultant can handle the ESMA process, which typically takes 1-2 weeks for initial registration.
