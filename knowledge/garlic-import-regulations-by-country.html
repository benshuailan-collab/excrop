Garlic Import Regulations by Country: A Complete Guide

Documentation, phytosanitary, tariff, and customs clearance requirements for garlic imports in key markets including Indonesia, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Brazil, Nigeria, and Southeast Asia.

Quick Answer: Garlic imports require a phytosanitary certificate, certificate of origin, fumigation certificate, commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading. Specific requirements vary by country: Indonesia requires an Import Approval, Brazil requires an Import License, Saudi Arabia requires Arabic labeling, and Nigeria requires Form M and potentially SONCAP.

Why Import Regulations Matter

Every country has its own import regulations for agricultural products like garlic. These regulations cover phytosanitary requirements, customs documentation, tariff rates, and sometimes quotas or seasonal restrictions. Failure to comply with import regulations can result in shipment delays, fines, or even cargo destruction. Understanding your country's requirements before placing an order is essential for smooth customs clearance.

Common Documentation Required

While specific requirements vary by country, the following documents are standard for garlic imports from China:

Document Issued By Purpose
Phytosanitary Certificate China Customs Confirms garlic is free from pests and diseases

Regulations by Key Import Markets

Indonesia

Indonesia is one of China's largest garlic export destinations. Importers must obtain an Import Approval (Surat Persetujuan Impor / SPI) from the Indonesian Ministry of Agriculture. Garlic imports require a phytosanitary certificate and certificate of origin. Indonesia applies a specific tariff on garlic imports, and import quotas may be in effect. See our Indonesia market page for more details.

Saudi Arabia and UAE

Middle Eastern countries require phytosanitary certificates and fumigation certificates. Saudi Arabia's SFDA (Saudi Food and Drug Authority) has specific labeling requirements in Arabic. The UAE requires food product registration through the Dubai Municipality. Both countries apply a 5% customs duty on garlic imports. See our Saudi Arabia and UAE market pages.

Brazil

Brazil requires an Import License (LI) from the Ministry of Development, Industry and Foreign Trade before shipment. Phytosanitary certificates are mandatory. Brazil applies a 14% tariff on garlic imports from China, though MERCOSUR trade agreements may affect rates for some origins. See our Brazil market page.

Nigeria and South Africa

Nigeria requires a Form M (import declaration), phytosanitary certificate, and certificate of origin. SONCAP (Standards Organization of Nigeria Conformity Assessment Program) certification may be required. South Africa requires a phytosanitary import permit from the Department of Agriculture, Land Reform and Rural Development. See our Nigeria and South Africa market pages.

Southeast Asia (Malaysia, Philippines, Vietnam)

These countries generally require phytosanitary certificates and certificate of origin. Malaysia requires SIRIM certification for some food products. The Philippines requires an Import Permit from the Bureau of Plant Industry. Vietnam requires phytosanitary inspection upon arrival. See our Malaysia, Philippines, and Vietnam market pages.

Tips for Smooth Customs Clearance

  • Obtain all required import permits and licenses before placing your order.
  • Work with an experienced customs broker in your destination country.
  • Ensure all documents are consistent (product description, quantity, and value must match across all documents).
  • Request document copies from your supplier before the vessel departs so you can prepare for import clearance.
  • Check if your country requires pre-shipment inspection (SGS, Bureau Veritas, Intertek).
  • Verify labeling requirements — some countries require labels in the local language.
  • Allow 2-5 extra days for customs clearance in your delivery timeline.

Frequently Asked Questions

What documents do I need to import garlic from China?
Standard documents include: phytosanitary certificate (from China Customs), certificate of origin (from CCPIT), fumigation certificate, commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading. Some countries require additional documents such as import permits, health certificates, or pre-shipment inspection reports. Check with your local customs authority for country-specific requirements.
Does garlic need fumigation before export?
Most countries require garlic shipments to be fumigated to prevent the spread of pests. Methyl bromide fumigation is the most common method, though some countries accept heat treatment alternatives. The fumigation certificate must be issued by a licensed fumigation company and included with the export documentation.
What is the import duty on garlic in my country?
Import duties on garlic vary significantly by country. Saudi Arabia and UAE apply approximately 5%, Brazil applies around 14%, and Indonesia has specific tariff rates that may change. Some countries have free trade agreements with China that may reduce or eliminate duties. Consult your customs broker or local customs authority for current tariff rates.
