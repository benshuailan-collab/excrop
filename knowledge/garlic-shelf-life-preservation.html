Garlic Shelf Life & Preservation: Maximizing Freshness for Export

How to maximize garlic shelf life through proper temperature, humidity, ventilation, cold chain management, and quality monitoring throughout the export supply chain.

Quick Answer: Fresh garlic stored at -1 to -3 degrees Celsius with 65-70% humidity has a shelf life of 6-9 months. At room temperature (20-25C), shelf life is only 2-4 weeks. Dehydrated garlic lasts 12-24 months in sealed packaging at ambient temperature. Key factors: temperature, humidity, ventilation, and skin integrity.

Understanding Garlic Shelf Life

Garlic shelf life depends on several factors: variety, storage conditions, post-harvest handling, and whether the garlic is fresh or dehydrated. For garlic exporters and importers, understanding these factors is critical for maintaining product quality throughout the supply chain and avoiding losses due to spoilage.

Storage Method Temperature Shelf Life
Cold storage (professional) -1 to -3°C 6-9 months
Refrigerated (commercial) 0-4°C 3-5 months
Cool, dry storage 10-15°C 1-3 months
Ambient (room temp) 20-25°C 2-4 weeks
Dehydrated garlic Ambient (dry) 12-24 months

Key Factors Affecting Garlic Shelf Life

Temperature

Temperature is the most critical factor. Garlic stored at -1 to -3 degrees Celsius enters a state of suspended animation where metabolic processes slow dramatically, extending shelf life to 6-9 months. At temperatures above 4 degrees Celsius, garlic begins to sprout, which reduces quality and shelf life. Avoid temperatures between 4 and 18 degrees Celsius, as this range actively promotes sprouting.

Humidity

Relative humidity should be maintained at 65-70%. Too high (above 75%) promotes mold and fungal growth, especially on damaged bulbs. Too low (below 60%) causes the garlic to lose moisture, leading to weight loss, shriveling, and loose skins. In cold storage facilities, humidity is controlled by ventilation systems that balance moisture levels.

Ventilation

Proper air circulation is essential in cold storage. Garlic produces small amounts of carbon dioxide and ethylene during storage. Without adequate ventilation, these gases accumulate and can accelerate deterioration. Cold storage facilities should maintain continuous air exchange to remove respiratory gases and maintain uniform temperature and humidity throughout the storage space.

Variety and Skin Integrity

Purple skin garlic generally has a slightly longer shelf life than pure white garlic due to its thicker, more protective outer skin. Regardless of variety, garlic with intact, tight outer skin stores better than garlic with damaged or loose skin. During sorting and packing, damaged bulbs should be removed to prevent them from affecting the surrounding healthy bulbs.

Cold Chain Management During Transport

Maintaining the cold chain during shipping is critical for preserving garlic quality. Reefer (refrigerated) containers should be pre-cooled to -1 to -3 degrees Celsius before loading. The container's ventilation vents should be partially open (typically 10-15 cubic meters per hour) to allow gas exchange. During transit, the reefer unit maintains the set temperature automatically.

Key cold chain practices:

  • Pre-cool the reefer container for at least 2 hours before loading
  • Set temperature to -1°C with a tolerance of +/-0.5°C
  • Set relative humidity to 65-70%
  • Keep ventilation vents at 10-15 m3/hr
  • Load containers promptly to minimize exposure to ambient temperature
  • Monitor reefer temperature data throughout transit

Signs of Quality Deterioration

During storage or upon arrival, inspect garlic for the following quality issues:

  • Sprouting — Green shoots emerging from the top. Indicates the garlic was stored at too high a temperature. Quality is reduced but still usable.
  • Mold — Blue-green or black mold on the skin or between cloves. Indicates excessive moisture. Affected bulbs should be removed immediately.
  • Soft or spongy cloves — Indicates rot or water damage. Reject affected bulbs.
  • Loose skins — Natural dehydration over time. Not a quality issue but reduces visual appeal.
  • Weight loss — Normal during storage. Expect 2-4% weight loss per month in cold storage.

Extending Shelf Life After Arrival

Once garlic arrives at the destination port, importers should transfer it to cold storage as quickly as possible. If professional cold storage is not available:

  • Store in the coolest available space (ideally below 10°C)
  • Maintain good ventilation to prevent gas buildup
  • Keep relative humidity between 60-70%
  • Avoid direct sunlight and heat sources
  • Do not store garlic in plastic bags (traps moisture and promotes mold)
  • Sell or distribute garlic within 2-4 weeks if stored at ambient temperature

For dehydrated garlic products, shelf life is much simpler: store in a cool, dry place in sealed packaging for 12-24 months. See our Dehydrated Garlic Applications Guide for more information.

Frequently Asked Questions

What temperature should garlic be stored at?
Garlic should be stored at -1 to -3 degrees Celsius (26-27 degrees Fahrenheit) for maximum shelf life of 6-9 months. Relative humidity should be 65-70%. Avoid the 4-18 degree Celsius range, as this temperature promotes sprouting. If professional cold storage is not available, store garlic in the coolest, driest place possible.
How long does garlic last at room temperature?
At room temperature (20-25 degrees Celsius), fresh garlic has a shelf life of only 2-4 weeks. In cool, dry conditions (10-15 degrees Celsius), it can last 1-3 months. For commercial importers, professional cold storage at -1 to -3 degrees Celsius is strongly recommended to maintain quality during storage and distribution.
How do I know if garlic has gone bad?
Signs of garlic deterioration include: green sprouts emerging from the top (sprouting), blue-green or black mold on the skin, soft or spongy cloves, and an unusual or fermented smell. Sprouted garlic is still usable but has reduced quality. Moldy, soft, or fermented garlic should be discarded. Loose skins are a normal sign of dehydration and not a quality issue.
