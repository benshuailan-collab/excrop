Ramadan Garlic Import Timeline: When Middle East Buyers Should Order from China

A back-planned month-by-month timeline for Saudi and UAE garlic buyers: locking cold-storage prices in October, sailing from Qingdao in December, and landing stock 2-3 weeks before Ramadan — while avoiding the Chinese New Year shipping squeeze.

Published: October 11, 2026 | Reading time: 12 min

Quick Answer: Garlic demand across the Middle East surges in the 3-4 weeks before Ramadan as households and restaurants build stocks. To have garlic on the shelf for that window, work backwards from the fasting month: enquire and lock prices in October-November (cold-storage season lows in Shandong), place orders by November-December, sail from Qingdao between mid-December and early January (20-28 days transit to Jeddah, Dammam, and Jebel Ali), and clear and warehouse cargo through January so stock is ready 2-3 weeks before the fasting month begins. Waiting until January-February means competing with the pre-Chinese-New-Year export rush and rising seasonal garlic prices.

Every importer in the Gulf food trade knows Ramadan moves the market. What many get wrong is how early the move starts. By the time Ramadan demand is visible in wholesale markets in Jeddah, Dubai, and Riyadh, the garlic that will satisfy it should already be on the water — because a container loaded in Qingdao today is 20-28 days from a Gulf port, and another week or more from a cleared, warehouse-ready position.

This guide walks through the demand mechanics, then builds a concrete back-planned timeline using the upcoming Ramadan (expected to begin around mid-February 2027, subject to moon sighting) as the working example. The same logic applies every year with the dates shifted. Why Garlic Demand Spikes Before Ramadan

During Ramadan, daily eating compresses into two main occasions — iftar (the evening meal that breaks the fast) and suhoor (the pre-dawn meal). Both are heavily centered on home cooking at scale. Families that normally cook on working-day rhythms move to large, communal meals every single evening for a month, and restaurants, catering companies, and hotel kitchens run banquets at maximum volume.

Garlic sits at the base of nearly every dish in the region's cuisines — kabsa, mandi, machboos, and the region-wide garlic sauces and marinades. Household purchasing shifts from small quantities to bulk stocking in the week before the month begins, and supermarket chains run promotions on fresh produce 2-3 weeks ahead of the start.

  • Households: Pre-Ramadan bulk shopping typically begins 1-2 weeks before the fasting month, building demand ahead of the calendar.
  • Retailers and supermarkets: Chains need stock positioned in distribution centers 2-3 weeks before the start, so their purchase orders land even earlier.
  • Restaurants and catering: Commercial kitchens contract for the full month in advance; their suppliers (wholesalers) must hold stock from before day one.
  • Eid al-Fitr tail: The festival meals at the end of the month extend strong demand 3-5 days beyond the last fasting day.

The practical consequence: the garlic that sells at Ramadan prices must arrive, clear customs, and sit in local cold storage before the retail surge — not during it.

Why October-November Is the Price Window

Shandong garlic is harvested in June and moves into cold storage from July onward. Between October and December, the region's cold stores are full, and exporters compete for program business — this is typically when seasonal prices sit at or near their lowest levels of the storage year. As winter progresses, stocks deplete, remaining quality grades tighten, and prices generally trend upward into the new year. A buyer who fixes Q4 prices for a January-arriving cargo is usually better positioned than one who buys spot in January. For a fuller picture of seasonal price behavior, see our garlic price trends guide.

Contract structures vary: some buyers fix a price per size grade for a fixed volume; others fix price for the first container and set a re-pricing formula for follow-on shipments. Either way, the conversation should start in October — before the pre-Chinese-New-Year rush changes the market's tone.

The Shipping Leg: Qingdao to Gulf Ports

Garlic for the Middle East loads at Qingdao, Shandong's export gateway. Typical ocean transits from Qingdao are 20-25 days to Jeddah (Red Sea), 22-26 days to Dammam (Saudi Gulf coast), and 22-28 days to Jebel Ali (UAE), depending on the service, transshipment routing, and schedule reliability. Add 5-7 days in China for production, phytosanitary inspection, and container loading, and 3-7 days at destination for clearance, and a container ordered in mid-November can realistically be warehouse-ready by mid-to-late January.

Reefer containers must be set at -2°C to -3°C with appropriate ventilation for the entire voyage. Garlic shipped outside the cold chain sprouts quickly, and a sprouted container arriving in January is a distressed cargo by February. Our fresh garlic storage guide covers the temperature science, and the garlic freight costs guide breaks down how reefer rates move across the season.

The Double Squeeze: Chinese New Year Meets Ramadan

The 2027 calendar creates a logistics squeeze that deserves its own planning line. Chinese New Year 2027 falls in mid-February — immediately adjacent to the start of Ramadan. Two consequences follow:

  • Pre-CNY export peak: In the 2-3 weeks before Chinese New Year, every Chinese export sector competes for containers, reefer equipment, trucking, and vessel space. Reefer freight rates from Qingdao typically firm up during this window, and equipment shortages can roll bookings by a week or more.
  • Factory and port slowdown: During the CNY holiday itself, packing labor, inspection scheduling, and document processing in Shandong run at reduced capacity for roughly 1-2 weeks. A garlic order confirmed in late January may not physically load until mid-to-late February — inside Ramadan.

A container that misses the mid-December-to-early-January sailing window therefore risks arriving during the fasting month itself, when distribution is slower (shortened working hours at ports, customs, and warehouses) and when local buyers have already filled their needs. Late arrivals usually end up as discounted post-Ramadan clearance stock. This is why the October-December ordering window is not a suggestion — it is the difference between selling into the peak and selling into the trough.

Ramadan Garlic Buying Checklist

  • 1. Confirm volume by market channel in September-October. Estimate your Ramadan uplift (typically 20-40% above a normal month for garlic in Gulf markets) and split it between retail, wholesale, and food-service demand. Convert that into 40HQ container units (28MT each) so supplier conversations are concrete.
  • 2. Lock prices and payment terms by end of November. Get FOB Qingdao or CIF quotes for your exact size grades, agree payment structure (for a review of options, see our L/C and payment terms guide), and secure reefer space for December sailings.
  • 3. Pre-book destination cold storage and clearance. Gulf ports and cold stores are busy in January; reserve chamber space and brief your clearing agent with the document checklist (phytosanitary certificate, certificate of origin, invoice, packing list, B/L — plus SFDA or municipality requirements) before the vessel sails. Country-specific rules are covered in our regulations by country guide.
  • 4. Plan a second wave only if the first lands clean. If your January arrival clears on time, a February re-order shipped before CNY can capture in-Ramadan demand; but never make the second container your Ramadan base stock. The rule of thumb in this trade: the first container is for the season; the second is for the upside.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the latest I can order garlic from China for Ramadan 2027?

Ordering by mid-December 2026 gives a realistic margin: production and loading take 5-7 days, sailing from Qingdao to Jeddah or Jebel Ali takes 20-28 days, and clearance takes 3-7 days. Anything confirmed in January enters the pre-Chinese-New-Year congestion window and risks arriving during Ramadan itself. The comfortable window closes in November.

How much does garlic demand rise during Ramadan in the Middle East?

Importers typically plan for a 20-40% uplift over a normal month, driven by household bulk buying, restaurant banquets, and catering. The uplift begins 1-2 weeks before the fasting month and carries through Eid al-Fitr. Because local garlic production in the Gulf is minimal, virtually the entire uplift is served by imports, mostly from China.

Does Chinese New Year really affect garlic shipments to the Gulf?

Yes, materially. In 2027, Chinese New Year falls in mid-February, immediately before Ramadan. In the 2-3 weeks before CNY, Qingdao reefer space tightens and rates typically rise across all export categories; during the holiday itself, packing, inspection, and documentation run at reduced capacity for 1-2 weeks. Ramadan cargo must sail before this squeeze, which is why December-early-January sailings are the target.

Should I buy Normal White or Pure White garlic for Ramadan?

For retail and supermarket programs, Pure White in 5.5cm-6.5cm is the market preference in Saudi Arabia and the UAE — its clean, bright appearance performs well in Ramadan displays and supports premium pricing. Normal White 5.0-5.5cm works for price-led wholesale channels. Many importers run both: Pure White for retail and Normal White for the wholesale volume.

How do I keep garlic fresh through the voyage and pre-Ramadan storage?

Hold the cold chain at -2°C to -3°C from loading at Qingdao through destination cold storage, with correct ventilation settings in the reefer. Garlic handled this way stores for months; garlic that warms up in transit or at the port will sprout, and a sprouted cargo in January loses its Ramadan premium entirely.
