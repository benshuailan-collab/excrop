How to Import Garlic from China to Indonesia: Complete 2026 Guide

A practical, step-by-step guide for Indonesian garlic importers: Tanjung Priok and Tanjung Perak ports, 0% ASEAN-China FTA tariff with Form E, Ministry of Agriculture import permit, 14-18 day shipping, and full cost breakdown.

Published: August 12, 2026 | Reading time: 13 min

Quick Answer: To import garlic from China to Indonesia, you need an API importer identification number, an import permit from the Ministry of Agriculture (obtained through INATRACE), a Form E certificate of origin (for 0% ACFTA tariff), and a Phytosanitary Certificate from your Chinese supplier. Ocean freight from Qingdao to Tanjung Priok (Jakarta) takes just 14-18 days — one of the shortest routes from China. With Form E, garlic qualifies for 0% preferential tariff under the ASEAN-China Free Trade Area. Indonesia prefers Normal White garlic in 5.0-5.5cm sizes. The standard MOQ is 1 x 40HQ Reefer Container (28MT) at -2°C to -3°C.

Indonesia is Southeast Asia's largest economy and one of the world's biggest garlic importers. With a population of over 275 million and garlic being an essential ingredient in Indonesian cuisine — from sambal to nasi goreng to various regional dishes — national consumption is enormous. Despite some domestic garlic production in the highlands of Java and Sumatra, local supply covers only a small fraction of demand. The vast majority of Indonesia's garlic comes from China, supported by the ASEAN-China Free Trade Area (ACFTA) agreement that enables 0% tariff on qualifying goods.

This guide is written for Indonesian trading companies, supermarket procurement teams, and food processors who want to source garlic directly from China. It covers the Indonesian garlic market, the ACFTA tariff advantage, the Ministry of Agriculture import permit process, shipping routes, documentation, and a cost breakdown comparing FOB Qingdao and CIF Jakarta terms.

Indonesia Garlic Market Overview

Indonesia imports approximately 450,000-550,000 metric tons of garlic annually, making it one of the world's largest garlic importers. Domestic production is estimated at only 30,000-40,000 MT per year, concentrated in the highland areas of Central Java (Tegal, Brebes), West Java, and parts of Sumatra. The gap of over 400,000 MT is filled almost entirely by imports from China, which benefits from the ACFTA 0% tariff and short shipping distances.

Garlic is deeply embedded in Indonesian culinary culture. It is a foundational ingredient in almost every Indonesian kitchen, used in spice pastes (bumbu), sambal, soups, fried rice, and meat dishes. Per capita garlic consumption in Indonesia is among the highest in Southeast Asia. Demand is relatively stable year-round, with slight increases during Ramadan and Eid al-Fitr, when home cooking intensifies.

The garlic distribution network in Indonesia is centered on two main port cities: Jakarta (Java's western hub) and Surabaya (Java's eastern hub). From these ports, garlic flows to traditional markets (pasar tradisional) in every major city, modern supermarket chains (Indomaret, Alfamart, Carrefour/Transmart), and food processing companies. The Brebes region in Central Java, known as the "city of shallots," also serves as a significant garlic trading hub due to its agricultural trading infrastructure.

Step-by-Step Import Process

Importing garlic from China to Indonesia involves seven key steps. The ACFTA tariff advantage and short shipping distance make this one of the more efficient garlic import routes, but the Ministry of Agriculture import permit requirement adds a regulatory step. Below is the complete process.

A supplier familiar with the Indonesian market will understand the ACFTA Form E requirements, the preferred garlic specifications, and the Ministry of Agriculture import permit process. Look for suppliers with ISO 22000 or HACCP certification, cold storage capacity, and a verifiable export track record to Southeast Asia.

Critically, the supplier must be able to obtain a Form E certificate of origin, which is the key document for claiming the 0% ACFTA tariff. Step 3: Understand Import Regulations & Tariffs

Indonesia regulates garlic imports through several government bodies. The key regulatory framework includes:

  • ACFTA 0% tariff with Form E: Under the ASEAN-China Free Trade Area agreement, garlic (HS code 0703.20.00) qualifies for 0% preferential import duty when accompanied by a valid Form E certificate of origin. Without Form E, the standard MFN (Most Favored Nation) rate of approximately 15-20% applies. This makes Form E one of the most important documents in the Indonesian garlic import process.
  • Value Added Tax (VAT): Indonesia applies a 11% PPN (Pajak Pertambahan Nilai) on imported goods, calculated on the customs value plus duty. Even with 0% duty under ACFTA, the 11% VAT still applies.
  • Ministry of Agriculture import permit: Fresh garlic requires an import permit (Izin Impor) from the Kementerian Pertanian (Ministry of Agriculture), issued through the INATRACE electronic system. The permit must be obtained before the shipment departs China.
  • API and NIB: Importers must have an API (Angka Pengenal Importir) importer identification number and a NIB (Nomor Induk Berusaha) business registration number. The API-U (General) is for general importers, while API-P (Producer) is for manufacturers importing for production use.
  • Indonesian Quarantine Agency: The Badan Karantina Indonesia (Barantan) oversees phytosanitary inspection of plant products at ports. All garlic shipments are subject to quarantine inspection upon arrival.

Step 4: Arrange Shipping & Logistics

Garlic is shipped from Qingdao Port to Indonesia in 40HQ reefer containers. The 14-18 day transit is one of the shortest garlic shipping routes from China, which reduces freight costs and cold-chain risk compared to routes to the Middle East, Africa, or South America.

The main destination ports for garlic in Indonesia are:

  • Tanjung Priok (Jakarta): Indonesia's busiest port, handling the majority of the country's containerized imports. Located in North Jakarta, it serves the densely populated Jakarta metropolitan area (Jabodetabek) and western Java. This is the primary entry point for garlic imports, with excellent connections to wholesale markets like Pasar Induk Kramat Jati.
  • Tanjung Perak (Surabaya): The second-busiest port in Indonesia, serving eastern Java and the eastern Indonesian archipelago. Importers serving East Java, Bali, and eastern Indonesia may prefer Tanjung Perak to reduce inland transport costs from Jakarta.

Major shipping lines on the Qingdao-Indonesia route include COSCO, OOCL, and Maersk. COSCO offers the most frequent direct services to both Tanjung Priok and Tanjung Perak. OOCL provides reliable transit times. Maersk offers comprehensive coverage. Most services are direct or involve a single transshipment at Singapore or Hong Kong. Book reefer containers in advance, especially during peak import periods before Ramadan and Eid.

The reefer container must be pre-cooled and set to -2°C to -3°C with ventilation at 25-30 CBM per hour. Indonesia's tropical climate means that upon arrival, garlic must be promptly moved to cold storage or distributed quickly. For cold-chain best practices, see our fresh garlic storage guide.

Step 5: Prepare Required Documents

Indonesia requires a comprehensive document set for customs clearance. The Form E certificate of origin is the most critical document for claiming the 0% ACFTA tariff. Missing or incorrect Form E will result in the standard MFN duty being applied, significantly increasing costs. The required documents include:

  • Commercial Invoice: Must include full seller and buyer details, product description, HS code, quantity, unit price, total value, Incoterms, and currency.
  • Packing List: Must show net weight, gross weight, number of packages, and container details.
  • Bill of Lading (B/L): Issued by the shipping line. Must be consistent with the commercial invoice and packing list.
  • Form E (Certificate of Origin): The ASEAN-China FTA certificate of origin, issued by CCPIT or the Entry-Exit Inspection and Quarantine Bureau (CIQ) in China. This is the key document for the 0% tariff. Ensure all fields are correctly filled, the HS code matches, and the certificate is original.
  • Phytosanitary Certificate: Issued by China Customs (GACC). Mandatory for all plant product imports into Indonesia.
  • Fumigation Certificate: Confirms pest treatment, required for phytosanitary compliance.
  • Ministry of Agriculture import permit (Izin Impor): Obtained through the INATRACE system before shipment. The permit number must be referenced in shipping documents.
  • API and NIB: The importer's API (importer identification) and NIB (business registration) numbers must be included in the customs declaration.

Step 6: Customs Clearance in Indonesia

Upon arrival at Tanjung Priok or Tanjung Perak, the shipment enters customs clearance through the CEISA (Customs Excise Information System and Automation) electronic system. The importer's customs broker files the customs declaration (PIB - Pemberitahuan Impor Barang), referencing all shipping documents, the Form E, the import permit, and the API/NIB numbers.

Indonesian Customs (Bea Cukai) uses a risk-based channel system: green (immediate release), yellow (document review), and red (physical inspection). Garlic shipments are frequently routed to the yellow or red channel due to the agricultural nature of the product. The Indonesian Quarantine Agency (Barantan) also conducts phytosanitary inspection, which may involve opening the container and sampling the garlic.

If the Form E is valid and all documents are in order, the 0% ACFTA tariff is applied, and only the 11% PPN (VAT) is payable. If the Form E is missing, invalid, or contains errors, the standard MFN duty (approximately 15-20%) applies — a costly mistake. Work with a customs broker experienced in ACFTA claims to ensure Form E compliance. After duty payment and inspection, customs issues a release letter, and the container can be picked up.

Step 7: Cold Storage & Distribution

After customs release, garlic should be moved to cold storage or distributed quickly. Indonesia's tropical climate — with temperatures of 30-35°C and high humidity year-round — is challenging for garlic storage. Jakarta and Surabaya have commercial cold storage facilities, but capacity can be limited. For traditional market distribution, garlic is often moved quickly from port to wholesale markets to minimize storage time.

In Jakarta, the primary wholesale market for garlic is Pasar Induk Kramat Jati, which supplies traditional markets (pasar tradisional) across the Jakarta metropolitan area. In Surabaya, Pasar Induk Wonokromo serves a similar function. From these hubs, garlic is distributed to smaller markets, supermarkets, and food processors across Java and the broader Indonesian archipelago.

For the food processing industry, which produces garlic paste, powder, and seasoning mixes, consistent quality and supply are essential. Dehydrated garlic flakes are also imported for industrial use — see our dehydrated garlic flakes product page for more information.

Indonesia-Specific Requirements & Regulations

Indonesia has several import requirements that are specific to its regulatory environment:

  • Form E accuracy: The Form E certificate of origin must be original, correctly filled, and the HS code must match the customs declaration. Common errors include wrong HS codes, missing signatures, or incorrect origin criteria. Any error can result in the 0% tariff being denied and the MFN rate applied.
  • Import permit timing: The Ministry of Agriculture import permit must be obtained before the shipment departs China. Apply through INATRACE well in advance, as processing can take 1-3 weeks. The permit has a validity period, so coordinate with your supplier's shipping schedule.
  • Indonesian language labeling: Pre-packaged garlic for retail sale must have labels in Indonesian, including product name, country of origin, net weight, and importer information. Bulk garlic for wholesale does not require retail labels.
  • Quarantine inspection: The Indonesian Quarantine Agency (Barantan) inspects all plant product imports. If quarantine pests or diseases are found, the shipment may be treated (at the importer's expense), re-exported, or destroyed.
  • Horticultural import regulations: Indonesia periodically updates its horticultural import regulations, which can affect garlic. Importers should monitor regulations from the Ministry of Trade and Ministry of Agriculture for any changes to import permit requirements or quota systems.

Shipping Routes: Qingdao to Tanjung Priok

The Qingdao-to-Tanjung Priok route is one of the shortest and most efficient garlic shipping lanes from China. The proximity of China and Indonesia, combined with frequent sailings from multiple carriers, makes logistics straightforward.

Garlic is loaded into a 40HQ reefer container at Qingdao Port. The container is connected to reefer power on the vessel and maintained at -2°C to -3°C throughout the voyage. Most services route south through the East China Sea and South China Sea, with some involving a transshipment at Singapore or Hong Kong. Direct services are available with COSCO and OOCL, reducing transit time.

Route Segment Transit Time

South China Sea to Tanjung Priok 8-12 days (may include Singapore transshipment)
Total ocean transit (Qingdao to Tanjung Priok) 14-18 days

COSCO offers the most frequent direct services to both Tanjung Priok and Tanjung Perak, with competitive transit times. OOCL provides reliable service with good reefer container availability. FAQ: Importing Garlic from China to Indonesia

Can I import garlic from China to Indonesia at 0% tariff?

Yes. Under the ASEAN-China Free Trade Area (ACFTA) agreement, garlic qualifies for 0% preferential tariff when accompanied by a valid Form E certificate of origin. Without Form E, the standard MFN tariff of approximately 15-20% applies. Ensure your Chinese supplier obtains Form E from CCPIT or CIQ before shipment, and verify that all fields are correctly filled to avoid tariff denial at Indonesian customs.

What documents are required to import garlic into Indonesia?

You need a Commercial Invoice, Packing List, Bill of Lading, Phytosanitary Certificate from China Customs, Form E (for ACFTA 0% tariff), fumigation certificate, and an import permit from the Ministry of Agriculture (obtained through INATRACE). You also need an API (importer identification number) and NIB (business registration). All documents must be consistent to avoid customs delays.

How long does it take to ship garlic from China to Indonesia?

Ocean freight from Qingdao Port to Tanjung Priok (Jakarta) takes 14-18 days — one of the shortest garlic shipping routes from China. Including 5-7 days for production, inspection, and loading in China, plus 2-4 days for customs clearance, the total lead time is typically 3-4 weeks. Tanjung Perak (Surabaya) adds 1-2 days to the transit time.

Which garlic specifications are most popular in the Indonesian market?

Indonesian buyers prefer Normal White garlic in 5.0cm and 5.5cm sizes. The market is price-sensitive, and Normal White garlic offers the best value for the high-volume Indonesian trade. Packaging is typically 20kg mesh bags for traditional market distribution or 10kg cartons for supermarket chains. Indonesia's large population and extensive culinary use of garlic create steady, year-round demand.

Do I need an import permit from the Ministry of Agriculture to import garlic into Indonesia?

Yes. Indonesia requires an import permit (Izin Impor) from the Ministry of Agriculture (Kementerian Pertanian) for fresh garlic. The permit is issued through the INATRACE electronic system and must be obtained before the shipment departs China. Importers must have a valid API and NIB. The permit process typically takes 1-3 weeks, so apply early and coordinate with your supplier's shipping schedule.
